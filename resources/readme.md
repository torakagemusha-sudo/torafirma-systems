### Mathematics for Systems Research and Engineering

is a public, **self-directed curriculum** for *developers, engineers, researchers,* and independent practitioners working across *scientific computing, artificial intelligence, control systems, simulation, and mathematical systems design*.

The guide presents mathematics as a working engineering discipline rather than a collection of isolated subjects. It develops a progression from proof and discrete structures through linear algebra, probability, dynamical systems, geometry, functional analysis, numerical methods, and computational experimentation.

**Particular emphasis is placed on:**

- translating between mathematical, computational, and physical representations;
- distinguishing intuition, numerical evidence, derivation, and proof;
- identifying assumptions, invariants, failure modes, and claim boundaries;
- designing reproducible numerical experiments;
- recognising where analogies are structurally valid and where they break down;
- building the mathematical judgement required for complex systems research.

The curriculum includes multiple study pathways, competency criteria, practical evidence templates, and a continuous synthesis practicum.

**It is intended as a rigorous public reference and research-development framework, not as an accredited qualification or substitute for formal professional certifications.**
